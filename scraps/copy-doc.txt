Property Valuation in Dublin and Leinster
An independent, evidence-based market valuation of your property — from a practice that values property for a living and has nothing to sell you afterwards.
What is a property valuation?
A property valuation is a professional assessment of what a property would sell for on the open market at a specific date. It is not an asking price, and it is not an estate agent&apos;s estimate of what might be achievable with the right campaign. It is a considered figure, supported by evidence, that can be relied upon for a financial or legal decision.
Our valuations are prepared to European Valuation Standards and set out not only the figure but the comparable sales, market conditions and property characteristics that produced it.
When do you need one?
Buying or selling and wanting an independent view free of any sales agenda
Applying for or restructuring a loan secured on property
Transferring property between family members or dissolving joint ownership
Establishing a value for tax purposes, including Capital Acquisitions Tax and Capital Gains Tax
Insurance, accounting, or company and pension asset valuation
Settling a dispute where an impartial figure is needed
What your valuation includes
A full internal and external inspection of the property
A written report stating the open market value as at a defined date
The comparable sales evidence the valuation is based on
Commentary on the property&apos;s condition, accommodation and any factors materially affecting value
A report signed by a PSRA-licensed, IPAV-registered valuer holding TEGoVA REV accreditation
Why choose an independent valuer?
An estate agent valuing your home is, in most cases, also pitching for the instruction to sell it. There is nothing improper about that, but it does mean the figure serves two purposes at once. When the valuation has to satisfy a lender, a solicitor or Revenue, that ambiguity is a problem.
JWL does not sell property. We produce a figure and we show you the evidence for it. That is the entire service.

What is a bank or credit union valuation?
When a lender advances money secured against property, it needs an independent professional assessment of what that security is worth. That is a loan security valuation, and it is a different document from a general market valuation: it follows the lender&apos;s format, states the value on a defined basis, and comments on anything that could affect the property&apos;s suitability as security.
Credit unions have grown substantially as mortgage and home improvement lenders in recent years, and their valuation requirements are broadly similar to the banks&apos;. We prepare reports for both.

Probate Valuation
Probate Property Valuations in Dublin and Leinster
Accurate, well-evidenced date-of-death valuations for probate and estate administration — prepared for solicitors, executors and personal representatives.
What is a probate valuation?
A probate valuation establishes the open market value of a property as at the date of the owner&apos;s death. That figure is required for the Statement of Affairs (Probate) Form SA.2 submitted to Revenue, and it forms the basis on which the estate is administered and any Capital Acquisitions Tax is calculated.
The date matters more than people expect. The valuation is not what the house is worth today — it is what it was worth on a specific past date, which may be months or even years ago. Getting that right requires evidence from the market as it stood then, not as it stands now.
Why the figure needs to be defensible
A probate valuation is submitted to Revenue and relied upon by beneficiaries. Under-value the property and the estate risks a Revenue query, an amended return and, potentially, interest and penalties. Over-value it and beneficiaries may pay tax on value that was never there. Where the property is later sold, the difference between the probate value and the sale price has capital gains consequences for the estate.
A figure that is simply asserted is difficult to defend if it is questioned. A figure supported by documented comparable evidence from around the valuation date is straightforward to defend, because the reasoning is on the page.
We understand the circumstances
Probate work almost always follows a bereavement, often recently. We keep the process as light as we reasonably can: we arrange the inspection at a time that suits the family, we do not need the house cleared or presented, and we deal directly with the solicitor on the paperwork if that is easier for you.
What your probate valuation includes
A full inspection of the property, arranged at your convenience
A written valuation stating open market value as at the date of death
Comparable sales evidence from around the valuation date, not today&apos;s market
A report in a format suited to Form SA.2 and to your solicitor&apos;s requirements
A signed report from a PSRA-licensed, IPAV-registered valuer

Family Law Property Valuations
Impartial, court-ready valuations for separation, divorce and judicial separation — evidence both sides and the court can rely on.
What is a family law valuation?
When a couple separates, the family home is usually the largest asset in the settlement. Before it can be divided, sold, transferred or offset against other assets, its value has to be established — and established in a way that both parties, their solicitors and, if it comes to it, the court will accept.
A family law valuation is an independent professional assessment of open market value, prepared to a documented methodology and set out in a report structured for legal proceedings.
Independence is the whole point
In a separation, a valuation that appears to favour one party is worse than no valuation at all — it becomes another thing to argue about, and it costs both parties in legal fees.
We are frequently instructed jointly by both parties or by both solicitors, precisely because we have no stake in the outcome. We are not selling the house, we are not acting for either side, and our figure is what the evidence supports. We are equally comfortable being instructed by one party, and the report is prepared to the same standard either way.

Fair Deal Valuation
Fair Deal Scheme Property Valuations
Independent property valuations for the HSE Nursing Homes Support Scheme financial assessment — accurate, evidenced and prepared without delay.
What is a Fair Deal valuation?
The Nursing Homes Support Scheme, universally known as Fair Deal, helps meet the cost of long-term residential care. How much a person contributes is determined by a financial assessment of their income and assets — and for most applicants, the principal private residence is the largest asset in that assessment.
The HSE requires a market valuation of the property as part of that assessment. A Fair Deal valuation is an independent professional valuation prepared for that purpose.
Why the figure matters to the family
The valuation directly affects the weekly contribution towards care. An inaccurately high valuation means a family contributes more than the scheme actually requires of them, potentially for years. An inaccurately low one risks the application being queried and delayed at a point when the family needs a decision.
Neither outcome is acceptable, and neither is necessary. A valuation grounded in genuine comparable evidence is simply correct — and being correct is what makes it hold up.
We know the process is difficult
Fair Deal applications are made at hard times. A parent has usually just moved into care, or is about to, and the family is dealing with the emotional side of that while working through an unfamiliar application form with deadlines attached.
We keep our part of it simple. We arrange the inspection at a time that suits, there is no need to prepare or present the house in any particular way, and the valuation is provided in the form the HSE needs. If you are not sure what the HSE is asking for, ask us — we deal with these applications regularly.
What your valuation includes
A full inspection of the property at a time convenient to the family
A written valuation of open market value as at the date of inspection
Comparable sales evidence supporting the figure
A report formatted for the HSE financial assessment
A signed report from a PSRA-licensed, IPAV-registered valuer
Fair Deal valuation FAQs
Does the HSE require a professional valuation?
The financial assessment requires the market value of the applicant&apos;s assets, including property. A valuation from a qualified, licensed valuer is the most straightforward way to evidence it, and reduces the likelihood of the figure being queried.
How often is a Fair Deal valuation needed?
A valuation is required at application. Property assets may be reassessed in later years depending on the circumstances of the case, so an updated valuation is sometimes requested. Check the current requirements with the HSE or your solicitor, and we can advise on the valuation side.
The house is empty. Is that a problem?
Not at all. We simply need access. If keys are with a family member, a solicitor or an auctioneer, we will arrange it with them.
Can you deal with our solicitor?
Yes. Many Fair Deal applications are handled by a solicitor and we work with them directly.

Local Property Tax (LPT) Valuation
Local Property Tax (LPT) Valuations
Independent evidence to support your LPT band — for homeowners who want a self-assessment they can stand over.
What is an LPT valuation?
Local Property Tax is a self-assessed tax. Revenue does not tell you what your property is worth; you declare a valuation band, and you are responsible for that declaration being reasonable and defensible.
For most homes, a careful look at recent local sales makes the band obvious. For a good many others it does not — and those are the cases we are instructed on.
When homeowners get a professional LPT valuation
The property sits close to a band boundary, where the difference between two bands is real money each year
The property is unusual, and there are few or no genuine comparables — a period home, a converted building, a large site, or a house in an area that rarely trades
Revenue has queried the declared band and evidence is needed to support it
The property is held in an estate, a trust or a company, where the declaration needs to be supported by documentation
The owner is an executor or attorney declaring on someone else&apos;s behalf and wants the decision properly evidenced
Self-assessment means you carry the risk
Because LPT is self-assessed, an under-declared band is your exposure, not Revenue&apos;s error. If the declaration is queried, what protects you is being able to show that the band was arrived at reasonably, on the basis of evidence available at the valuation date.
That is precisely what a written valuation from an accredited valuer provides — a documented, dated record of the comparable evidence and the reasoning behind the band you declared.
What your LPT valuation includes
Inspection of the property, or a desktop assessment where that is appropriate to the case
A written valuation as at the relevant LPT valuation date
The comparable evidence supporting the figure and the band
A clear statement of the band the valuation places the property in
A signed report from a PSRA-licensed, IPAV-registered valuer

What if my property has changed since the valuation date?
The declaration relates to the property as it stood at the valuation date, so a later extension or refurbishment does not usually change the band for that period. We can advise on how your circumstances apply.

Historic Valuation
Historic and Retrospective Property Valuations
What was the property worth then? A properly evidenced valuation as at a specific past date, for tax, legal and accounting purposes.
What is a historic valuation?
A historic — or retrospective — valuation establishes what a property was worth at a defined date in the past rather than today. It is a distinct discipline: today&apos;s market is irrelevant, and the valuer has to reconstruct the market as it stood, using only evidence that existed at or around the relevant date.
Ireland&apos;s property market has moved dramatically over the last two decades. The gap between what a house was worth in 2007, in 2012 and today can be enormous, so the date is not a detail — it is the entire question.
When you need one
Capital Gains Tax, where the base cost is the value at acquisition or at a specified date
Capital Acquisitions Tax on a gift or inheritance taken at an earlier date
Probate where the date of death has passed some time ago, or an estate is being reopened
The 6 April 1974 CGT rebasing date, and other statutory valuation dates
Legal proceedings where value at a particular moment is in dispute
Correcting or supporting a return already filed with Revenue
Company accounts, pension schemes and trust accounting requiring a value as at a year end
How we do it
We assemble comparable evidence from the market as it stood at the valuation date — recorded transactions, published data and, where the date predates the Property Price Register, other contemporaneous sources. We then assess the subject property as it stood at that date, allowing for its condition and configuration at the time rather than as it is now.
Retrospective valuations attract scrutiny from Revenue more often than current ones, for obvious reasons. That is exactly why the evidence trail matters more here than anywhere else, and why our reports set the full trail out.
Historic valuation FAQs
How far back can you go?
We routinely value back to the 1990s and, where sufficient evidence is available, considerably further — including the 1974 CGT rebasing date. Tell us the date you need and we will tell you honestly what evidence exists for it.
Do you need to inspect the property?
Usually yes, because the current property informs our understanding of what was there before. Where access is impossible, we can prepare a desktop valuation from documentary evidence — we will tell you at the outset whether that is appropriate in your case and what its limitations are.
Will Revenue accept a retrospective valuation?
A valuation from a qualified, accredited valuer that documents its evidence and methodology is the appropriate way to support a return. We cannot speak for Revenue in any individual case, but a well-evidenced report is far easier to stand over than an unsupported figure.

Blue Book Valuation
Blue Book Valuations — European Valuation Standards
Valuation reports prepared to European Valuation Standards by a TEGoVA-accredited valuer — recognised by lenders and institutions across Ireland and Europe.
What is a Blue Book valuation?
The Blue Book is the property industry&apos;s name for European Valuation Standards, published by TEGoVA, the European Group of Valuers&apos; Associations. EVS sets out the definitions, bases of value, methodology and reporting requirements that a professional valuation should follow.
A Blue Book valuation is one prepared in accordance with those standards by a valuer accredited to apply them. In Ireland, TEGoVA&apos;s accreditations — Recognised European Valuer (REV) and TEGoVA Residential Valuer (TRV) — are awarded through IPAV, the only Irish institute with awarding rights.
Blue Book and Red Book
People often ask which standard they need. Both are recognised professional valuation frameworks: the Blue Book is TEGoVA&apos;s European Valuation Standards, applied across the EU and administered in Ireland through IPAV; the Red Book is the RICS Valuation Global Standards, applied through RICS and the SCSI.
They cover much the same ground and both produce valuations to a recognised professional standard. What matters in practice is what the party receiving the report requires. If your lender, solicitor or institution has specified one, tell us at the outset. If they have not specified, ask them — and if you would rather not, we are happy to ask on your behalf.
Why it matters
Working to a defined standard is what separates a professional valuation from an estimate. It means the basis of value is stated explicitly, the methodology is recognised, the assumptions are disclosed, and the report contains what a reader needs in order to test the conclusion.
That is the same idea as our tagline, expressed in a formal standard. Evidence before opinion is not just how we prefer to work; it is what EVS requires.

What is a switcher valuation?
When you move your mortgage from one lender to another, the new lender needs its own independent valuation of the property it is taking as security. It will not rely on the valuation done when you first bought, however recent that was.
A switcher valuation is a loan security valuation prepared for that purpose, in the new lender&apos;s format.
Switching is usually worth doing — and usually done badly
Irish homeowners leave a great deal of money on the table by staying with their original lender out of inertia. Over the remaining term of a mortgage, a rate difference of a fraction of a percent is a substantial sum.
Two things commonly go wrong. The first is delay: the valuation is left to the end, and the switch stalls waiting for it. The second is that people switch without checking whether their loan to value band has improved — if your property has risen in value or your balance has fallen, you may qualify for a materially better rate than the one you are being quoted. It is worth checking before you commit.

Rental Valuations and Market Rent Assessments
Independent, evidence-based assessments of open market rent — for landlords, tenants, rent reviews and disputes.
What is a rental valuation?
A rental valuation establishes the open market rent for a property: what it would reasonably let for, on the terms in question, at a stated date. Like a capital valuation, it is built from comparable evidence — in this case, comparable lettings — rather than from what a listing site suggests or what a landlord hopes to achieve.
Ireland&apos;s rental market operates under a substantial and evolving body of regulation, and the market rent question frequently arises in a regulatory or contractual context rather than a straightforward letting one. That is where an independent, evidenced assessment earns its keep.

Loan to Value (LTV) Valuation
Loan to Value (LTV) Valuations
If your property is worth more than when you bought it, an up-to-date valuation may support a move into a lower interest rate band. Several of the main lenders have a formal process for exactly this.
What is loan to value?
Loan to value is the size of your mortgage expressed as a percentage of the property&apos;s value. Borrow €300,000 against a €400,000 property and your LTV is 75%.
Most Irish lenders price their mortgage rates in LTV bands — typically breaking at 90%, 80%, 70%, 60% and 50%. The lower the band, the lower the risk to the lender, and the lower the rate on offer.
Why this is worth ten minutes of your attention
Your LTV falls over time for two reasons at once: you pay down the balance, and the property changes in value. Neither happens on your lender&apos;s initiative. Your rate is set against the LTV recorded when the loan was drawn down, and it generally stays there until somebody evidences a change.
That means a homeowner who bought several years ago may already be in a cheaper band and simply paying the older rate. The only thing standing between them and it is a current valuation showing where the property now sits.
Worked example: a mortgage of €270,000 on a property valued at €300,000 sits at 90% LTV. If the property is now worth €340,000 and the balance has fallen to €255,000, the LTV is 75% — two bands lower. Whether that translates into a lower rate depends on your lender and your product, but where it does, the saving over the remaining term is set against a single one-off valuation fee.
When to get an LTV valuation
Property prices in your area have risen since you bought
You have been paying down the mortgage for several years
You have extended, renovated or materially improved the property
You are switching lender and want to secure the best band available
You are coming off a fixed rate and choosing a new product
You are considering a top-up or equity release and need to know where you stand
Before you instruct anyone — check three things with your lender
An LTV band move is a formal process, and each lender runs it slightly differently. One phone call before you spend anything will tell you whether it is worth doing.
Does the valuer need to be on the lender&apos;s approved panel? Several of the main lenders will only accept a report from a valuer on their own list.
How recent must the valuation be? Lenders typically require a report dated within the last four to six months, so timing matters.
Are you on a fixed rate? If so, moving rate before the fixed period ends can trigger a breakage cost. Some lenders let you apply in the months before your fixed rate matures.

Article One
What a Probate Valuation Actually Involves — And Why the Date Matters More Than You Think
Executors are often told they need &quot;a valuation for probate&quot; without being told what that means, why the date is critical, or what happens if the figure is wrong. Here is the plain-English version.
If you have been appointed executor of an estate, one of the first practical tasks that lands on you is establishing what the deceased&apos;s property was worth. It sounds simple. It is where a surprising number of estates run into difficulty months later.
It is not what the house is worth today
A probate valuation states the open market value of the property as at the date of death. Not today. Not when the house eventually sells. On that specific date.
Where death occurred recently and the market has been stable, the distinction may make little practical difference. Where probate has been delayed — and delays of a year or more are common where a will is contested, assets are complicated or family members live abroad — it matters a great deal. A valuation carried out today, in today&apos;s market, is simply answering a different question from the one Revenue is asking.
Why the figure gets scrutinised
The probate value does more work than most executors realise. It goes into the Statement of Affairs (Probate) Form SA.2 submitted to Revenue. It determines each beneficiary&apos;s inheritance and their Capital Acquisitions Tax position. And if the property is subsequently sold, the difference between the probate value and the sale price is the gain — with capital gains tax consequences for the estate.
This creates a trap that catches people out. An executor, wanting to be helpful, accepts a conservative figure. The house then sells eighteen months later for considerably more. The estate now faces a capital gains liability that could have been reduced, and Revenue may reasonably ask whether the original valuation was realistic.
The reverse trap is just as real. An optimistic valuation means beneficiaries pay inheritance tax on value that was never actually there.
What makes a probate valuation defensible
The distinction that matters is not between a high figure and a low one. It is between a figure that is asserted and a figure that is evidenced.
An asserted valuation says the property was worth €X. If Revenue queries it, there is nothing behind it but the valuer&apos;s say-so.
An evidenced valuation says the property was worth €X, and here are the sales of similar properties nearby from around that date that support it. That is a document that can be tested — and that is why it tends to survive being tested.
What executors should ask for
A valuation stated as at the date of death, with the date set out explicitly in the report
Comparable evidence from around that date, not from the current market
A signed report from a qualified, PSRA-licensed valuer, with their credentials on it
A format your solicitor can work with for Form SA.2
A valuer willing to explain the reasoning if anyone later questions the figure
A note on the practical side
Executors often worry about the state of the house — that it is cluttered, that it has not been decorated in decades, that the garden has gone wild. None of that needs attention before a valuation. Condition is one of the things we are assessing, and it is reflected in the figure. There is nothing to prepare and nothing to apologise for.
The valuation is one of the more straightforward parts of administering an estate, provided it is done properly and once.

Article Two
Local Property Tax: How to Value Your Home for the Bands With Confidence
LPT is a self-assessed tax. Revenue does not tell you what your home is worth — you declare it, and you carry the responsibility for that declaration being reasonable.
Most homeowners treat their LPT return as a five-minute administrative job. For most homes, that is a fair assessment: the property is ordinary, the road has traded recently, the band is obvious, and the declaration is straightforward.
For a significant minority of properties, none of that is true — and those are the returns where a little care now saves considerable difficulty later.
Self-assessment means the risk sits with you
This is the point most often missed. Revenue does not issue you a valuation. You declare a band, and you are responsible for that declaration being reasonable and supportable.
If your declared band is later queried, the question will not be whether you got the number exactly right. It will be whether you arrived at it reasonably, on the basis of evidence available at the valuation date. Being able to answer that question is what a documented valuation gives you.
The valuation date is not today
LPT works on a fixed valuation date that applies for an entire valuation period, rather than on a rolling current value. Your declaration relates to what the property was worth on that date — so a rise in prices afterwards, or an extension built later, does not generally change your band for that period.
This trips people up in both directions. Some over-declare because they are thinking of today&apos;s market. Others assume a recent improvement pushes them up a band when, for the period in question, it does not.

When to be careful
Take extra care where:
Your property sits close to a band boundary. The difference between two bands is a real annual sum, and boundary cases are exactly where a casual estimate is most likely to be wrong.

How to arrive at a band you can stand over
Start with genuine comparable evidence. The Residential Property Price Register records actual sale prices, which is considerably more useful than asking prices on a listing site — asking prices are aspirations, and the gap between the two varies with the market.
Look for properties genuinely like yours: same type, similar size, same immediate area, sold as close as possible to the valuation date. Three good comparables beat twenty loose ones. Then adjust honestly for the differences — size, condition, BER, garden, aspect, parking, and the specific location within the area.
Write down what you did and keep it. If the band you reach is comfortably inside a band rather than sitting on a boundary, your work is probably done.
When to get a professional valuation
If you are on a boundary, if the property is unusual, if you are declaring for someone else, or if Revenue has already queried the band, a written valuation from an accredited valuer is a proportionate step. It gives you a dated, documented record of the evidence and reasoning behind the band you declared — which is precisely what you need if the declaration is ever examined.

Article Three
Could a Loan to Value Valuation Cut Your Mortgage Rate?
Your mortgage rate may be set against a loan to value figure recorded years ago. If your property has risen in value since, you could already qualify for a cheaper band — but nobody will tell you.
Most Irish lenders price mortgages in loan to value bands. Your LTV is the size of your mortgage as a percentage of the property&apos;s value, and the bands typically break around 90%, 80%, 70%, 60% and 50%. The lower your LTV, the less risk the lender carries, and the better the rate on offer.
Here is the part that costs homeowners money: your rate is generally set against the LTV recorded when the loan was drawn down. It does not adjust itself as your circumstances improve.
Your LTV improves in two ways at once
The first is obvious. Every repayment reduces the balance outstanding.
The second is the one people forget. The property&apos;s value changes independently of anything you do. Someone who bought in 2018 and has been paying down a mortgage since may be in a materially different band from the one their rate reflects — and paying accordingly.
What the difference looks like
Take a mortgage of €270,000 drawn down against a property valued at €300,000. That is a 90% LTV, and it will have been priced at 90% rates.
Some years on, the balance has fallen to €255,000 and the property is now worth €340,000. The LTV is 75% — two bands lower. If the lender&apos;s pricing rewards that with a lower rate, the saving over the remaining term of the mortgage is a significant sum, against a one-off valuation fee.
That gap does not close on its own. It closes when somebody evidences the change.
How to check whether it is worth doing
Three steps, and the first two are free.
One: find your current outstanding balance. It is on your most recent mortgage statement or in your online banking.
Two: call your lender and ask two questions — what LTV band your current rate is priced at, and what bands and rates are available on your product. Ask specifically whether they will re-band on the basis of an updated valuation, and whether the valuation must come from their panel.
Three: if the answers suggest a band change is realistically in reach, get an independent valuation. If they do not, you have spent one phone call finding out — which is a good outcome either way.
A word on honesty
It is worth being clear about something. A valuation might come back showing you are exactly where you thought, with no band change available. That is a legitimate result, and any valuer who implies otherwise is not someone you want writing a report you intend to rely on.
A valuation is worth having because it tells you what is true. If the answer is favourable, it can be worth a great deal. If it is not, you have paid a modest fee to stop wondering — and you know where you stand for the next time you look at your mortgage.
While you are at it
If you are looking at your LTV, look at switching too. The two questions belong together: the band your valuation puts you in affects the rate a new lender will offer, and a switch is often where the improved band actually gets realised.

H2: About valuations generally
What is a property valuation?
A professional assessment of what a property would sell for on the open market at a specific date, supported by comparable sales evidence and prepared to recognised valuation standards. It is a considered, evidenced figure — not an asking price or a marketing estimate.
What is the difference between a valuation and a survey?
A valuation establishes what a property is worth. A structural survey assesses its physical condition and identifies defects. They answer different questions and are separate instructions. Lenders frequently require both. JWL provides valuations.
How is a valuation different from an estate agent&apos;s appraisal?
An estate agent&apos;s appraisal is usually free, usually verbal, and usually connected to a pitch for the selling instruction. A formal valuation is an independent written report from a licensed valuer, setting out the evidence and reasoning, and prepared to be relied upon by a lender, a solicitor, a court or Revenue.
How long is a valuation valid for?
A valuation states a value as at a specific date. In practice, most lenders and institutions treat a report as current for around three to six months, though each sets its own requirement. If yours has expired, an update is usually quicker and cheaper than a fresh instruction.
What types of property do you value?
Houses, apartments, period properties, buy-to-let and investment properties, residential portfolios, sites and land — across Dublin and Leinster.

H2: Legal, probate and statutory valuations
Do you provide valuations for probate?
Yes. Probate valuations are a substantial part of our work. We value as at the date of death using comparable evidence from that period, in a format suited to Form SA.2, and we deal with your solicitor directly if that is easier.
Can you value a property as at a date in the past?
Yes. Retrospective valuation is routine for us, for CGT, CAT, probate and legal purposes, using evidence from the market as it stood at the relevant date.
Do you provide valuations for the Fair Deal Scheme?
Yes. We prepare independent valuations for the HSE Nursing Homes Support Scheme financial assessment, in the format the HSE expects.
Can both parties in a separation instruct you jointly?
Yes, and it is frequently the most efficient approach — a single agreed valuer removes one point of dispute and one set of fees. We are equally happy to be instructed by one party, and the report is prepared to the same standard either way.
Are your reports accepted by Revenue and the courts?
Our reports are prepared to European Valuation Standards by a PSRA-licensed, IPAV-registered valuer holding TEGoVA REV accreditation, and they document the evidence and methodology behind the figure. That is what makes a valuation capable of being relied upon and defended. We cannot speak for any individual decision-maker, but an evidenced report is considerably more robust than an unsupported figure.
H2: Lending, LTV and switching
Are your valuations accepted by banks and credit unions?
Yes. We prepare reports in lenders&apos; required formats and to European Valuation Standards. If your lender has a specific template or a panel requirement, tell us when you get in touch. [CLIENT TO CONFIRM: add the specific lender panels JWL is on — people search for these by name.]
Can a valuation reduce my mortgage rate?
It can. Most lenders price by loan to value band, so if your property has risen in value or your balance has fallen, a current valuation may support a move into a cheaper band. Before instructing anyone, ask your lender three things: whether they will re-band on an updated valuation, whether the valuer must be on their panel, and how recent the report has to be. That one call tells you whether it is worth doing.
Can I reuse an old valuation when switching mortgage?
Almost never. A new lender requires a current valuation instructed for its own purposes.
What is a Blue Book valuation?
A valuation prepared in accordance with European Valuation Standards, published by TEGoVA and known in the industry as the Blue Book. Our director, Shane Cooke, holds TEGoVA&apos;s REV accreditation, awarded in Ireland through IPAV.
